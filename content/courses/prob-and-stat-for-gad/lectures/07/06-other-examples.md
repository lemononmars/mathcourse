## ? Distribution

If the problem doesn't sound like any of those distributions, you might need to go back and start from scratch.

:::example

Suppose you have 4 aces, one of each suit. Your experiment is to draw a random card, without replacement, until you see the **second** red ace.

:::


:::solution

Simply find $P(X = k)$ for each $k$. For instance, $P(X=1) = 0$ since it is impossible to get the second red ace on your first draw!


| k | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P(X = k) | 0 | \frac{4}{24} | \frac{8}{24} | \frac{12}{24} |


Notice the longer you draw, the higher chance you will see the second ace. This doesn't follow any of our previous distributions.

:::
